\chapter{Conclusion}
\label{sec:07_conclusion_conclusion}

This internship investigated machine-learning surrogate models for collective-efefcts studies in the FCC-ee High-Energy Booster. Starting from the formulation of beam dynamics as distribution evolution under external and collective forces, the work developed a hierarchy of learned approximations: Fourier Neural Operators for reduced longitudinal profiles, a conditional variational representation for fifteen pairwise projections of six-dimensional beam clouds, Transformer-based latent dynamics, local-window attention, and Graph Neural Operator propagation.

The principal empirical result is that structured support is beneficial for the evaluated full-lattice endpoint task. Relative to dense causal attention, local-window attention reduces the observed reconstruction error, while the selected GNO checkpoint gives the lowest relative histogram error and neural inference time among the tested propagators. This conclusion concerns selected single-seed models and does not establish that the physical dynamics are strictly local. It also does not validate the internal element-indexed latent states: the available six-dimensional data supervise only the upstream and final downstream clouds, so arbitrarystep evolution and intermediate physical trajectories remain untested.

The standalone CVAE test error of \(0.367\) shows that the fifteen-marginal latent bottleneck already introduces non-negligible reconstruction loss before propagation. The full CVAE–GNO error of \(0.511\) therefore combines representation and endpoint-propagation effects. Moreover, pairwise projections do not identify three- and higher-coordinate correlations of a general non-Gaussian six-dimensional distribution. The present results support this representation as a practical reduced description, not as a unique reconstruction of the complete joint density.

The conditional-latent uncertainty study provides a further qualification. Posterior sampling produces spatially structured variability, but the ensemble spread is substantially smaller than the actual discrepancy: representative discrepancy-to-spread ratios are approximately \(19.6 , 55.5 ,\) and \(7.44\) under the reported diagnostics. These intervals are under-dispersed sensitivity indicators and must not be interpreted as calibrated predictive coverage.

The FNO results connect longitudinal density evolution with the wake-convolution and Haïssinski formulations, while the six-dimensional study identifies local attention and graph-kernel message passing as promising architectural biases. Future work should add simulator checkpoints along the lattice, repeat model comparisons over independent seeds, benchmark the pairwise representation against joint point-cloud or normalizing-flow models, impose relevant physical constraints explicitly, and calibrate predictive uncertainty. With these extensions, structured latent operators could support faster parameter scans, design-space exploration, and accelerator digital-twin workflows. Preliminary results from this research were published in the JACoW proceedings of IPAC \cite{ghribi2026updates}.
